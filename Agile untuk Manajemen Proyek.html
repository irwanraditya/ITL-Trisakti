Dua dunia dalam satu gedung

Business as usual · proyek baru · membangun bertahap

Bagian 1005 / 031

Analogi

Gedung kantor yang menambah lantai tanpa menutup kantornya

Gambarannya

Di lantai 1–3 pegawai tetap bekerja seperti biasa. Di atasnya, mandor dan tukang membangun lantai 4–6. Keduanya berjalan bersamaan, dipisahkan jaring pengaman.

Tarikannya ke materi

Lantai bawah adalah business as usual: rutin dan harus tetap stabil. Lantai atas adalah proyek baru: belum pasti dan terus berubah. Agile adalah cara membangun lantai atas tanpa mengganggu lantai bawah.

Di sini analoginya berhenti

Fondasi dan struktur gedung tidak bisa diubah sesuka hati. Proyek non-fisik (sistem, SOP, layanan baru) jauh lebih mudah dipecah dan diulang.

Bagian 1 · Simulasi007 / 031

Dua dunia

Business as usual mengejar kestabilan; proyek baru mengejar perubahan

Business as usual
  • Pekerjaan berulang setiap hari: kirim, terima, tagih
  • Ukuran: efisiensi, ketepatan waktu, biaya per unit
  • Perubahan dihindari karena mengganggu

Lantai 1–3: harus tetap jalan.

Proyek baru · new development
  • Sekali jalan, punya awal dan akhir
  • Ukuran: hasil baru yang bisa dipakai
  • Penuh ketidakpastian; perubahan pasti datang

Lantai 4–6: dibangun bertahap.

Jaring pengaman di antaranya: setiap hasil proyek dicek dulu sebelum menyentuh operasional.

Peran

Siapa melakukan apa di proyek gedung kita

Product Owner

Pemilik gedung

Menentukan prioritas dan menerima hasil setiap sprint.

Fasilitator · Scrum Master

Mandor

Menjaga ritme dan menghapus hambatan. Membantu, bukan memerintah.

Tim pelaksana · Developers

Tukang lintas keahlian

Besi, cor, bata, listrik dalam satu tim; menentukan sendiri cara kerjanya.

Pengguna · stakeholder

Penghuni lantai

Memakai hasil dan memberi masukan saat review sprint.

Di sini analoginya berhenti

Di lokasi proyek nyata, mandor sering memberi perintah langsung. Dalam Agile, tim menentukan cara kerjanya sendiri; fasilitator membantu tim, bukan mengatur setiap langkah.

CI · continuous integration

Integrasi berkelanjutan

Pekerjaan kecil sering digabung ke hasil utama dan langsung dicek, supaya masalah ketahuan saat masih kecil. Gedung: setiap cor kolom diuji sebelum lantai berikutnya.

CD · continuous delivery

Pengiriman berkelanjutan

Hasil yang lolos pengecekan selalu dalam keadaan siap diserahkan. Gedung: lantai yang lolos uji kelayakan langsung dibuka untuk penghuni.

Kapan cocok

Agile cocok untuk yang belum pasti; rencanakan penuh yang mahal diubah

Cocok bila
  • Kebutuhan belum pasti atau cepat berubah
  • Hasil bisa dipecah menjadi bagian yang sudah berguna
  • Pengguna bisa memberi masukan dengan cepat
  • Biaya mengubah arah rendah
Kurang cocok bila
  • Perubahan sangat mahal atau berbahaya: fondasi, struktur
  • Regulasi menuntut desain final sejak awal
  • Ketergantungan fisik ketat antartahap
  • Kontrak harga tetap dengan lingkup kaku

Two worlds in one building

Business as usual · new development · building in stages

Part 1005 / 031

Analogy

An office building adding floors without closing the office

The picture

On floors 1–3 staff keep working as usual. Above them, a foreman and crew build floors 4–6. Both run at once, separated by a safety deck.

The link to the topic

The lower floors are business as usual: routine and must stay stable. The upper floors are the new project: uncertain and changing. Agile is a way to build the upper floors without disturbing the lower ones.

Where the analogy stops

A building’s foundation and structure cannot be changed at will. Non-physical projects (systems, procedures, new services) are far easier to split and iterate.

Who does what in our building project

Product Owner

Building owner

Sets priorities and accepts the result of each sprint.

Facilitator · Scrum Master

Foreman

Keeps the rhythm and clears obstacles. Helps rather than commands.

Delivery team · Developers

Multi-skilled crew

Steel, concrete, brick and electrics in one team; decides how to do the work.

Users · stakeholders

Floor tenants

Use the result and give feedback at the sprint review.

Where the analogy stops

On a real site the foreman often gives direct orders. In Agile the team decides how it works; the facilitator helps the team rather than directing every step.

Part 4 · Backlog021 / 031

Wrong first, then right

Two ways to report progress on the same project

Percentage

“The Pontianak service project is 90% done.”

Ninety percent of what? Not one shipment has arrived. The last 10% often takes the longest.

Usable result

“The weekly route already serves 1 contract customer; app tracking follows next sprint.”

Checkable, already delivering value, and clear about what comes next.

CI · continuous integration

Continuous integration

Small pieces of work are merged into the main result often and checked at once, so problems surface while they are small. Building: every concrete column is tested before the next floor.

CD · continuous delivery

Continuous delivery

Anything that passes the checks is always ready to hand over. Building: a floor that passes inspection opens to tenants straight away.

Part 5 · Value026 / 031

When it fits

Agile fits the uncertain; plan in full what is costly to change

Fits when
  • Needs are unclear or change fast
  • Results can be split into parts that are useful on their own
  • Users can give feedback quickly
  • Changing direction is cheap
Fits poorly when
  • Change is very costly or dangerous: foundations, structure
  • Regulation demands a final design up front
  • Tight physical dependencies between stages
  • Fixed-price contracts with rigid scope
The middle way (hybrid): plan the foundation and structure in detail up front, then iterate everything built on top.
Part 5 · Limits027 / 031

Three things

Three habits that make any project agile

01

Small and often

Split the project into pieces finished in weeks.

02

Done means usable

Not 90%, but in use and delivering value.

03

Learn every cycle

Review with users, retrospective with the team, then adjust.

Summary028 / 031

Class activity · small groups

Split your project into three sprints

STEP 1

Choose

One real project at work or on campus, and who its users are.

STEP 2

Build a backlog

Six to eight items, ordered by value to users.

STEP 3

Plan 3 sprints

What is usable at the end of each sprint, and its definition of done.

One rule: by the end of Sprint 1 someone can already use something.
